## A Dwarf Fortress & Worldbox Inspired World-Building Expansion

## PART 1: THE DEEP HISTORY — AGES OF AETHEL

### The Age of Embers (Pre-History)

> *"Before the System, there was only fire and ash. The first beings crawled from the embers with nothing but hunger."*

**Epoch 1: The First Flame (10,000+ years ago)**
- The world was a barren wasteland of ash and stone.
- The First Flame appeared—not as a fire, but as a *concept*. The idea of warmth, of growth, of potential.
- From the First Flame, the first Ash-Kin emerged. They were not born—they *condensed* from the embers.
- The Ash-Kin wandered the ash wastes, driven by an instinct they did not understand: *to remember*.

**Epoch 2: The Birth of the Dungeon (8,000 years ago)**
- The Ash-Kin discovered a chasm in the earth—the Outer Fissure.
- They descended, and the Dungeon welcomed them. It taught them their first skills: *Sparks*, *Shadows*, and *Silence*.
- The first monsters were born—manifestations of the Dungeon's will to test.
- The Ash-Kin emerged changed. They carried the Dungeon's lessons back to the surface.

**Epoch 3: The Emergence of Beasts (6,000 years ago)**
- Monsters from the Dungeon began to spill onto the surface.
- Some monsters were killed. Some were captured. Some were studied.
- A few, through exposure to the System, *became something else*—the first Beastfolk.
- The Beastfolk were seen as abominations by the Ash-Kin. They were hunted, driven to the northern wastes.

---

### The Age of Forging (The System's Awakening)

> *"The System did not wake up. It was woken up. And it was hungry."*

**Epoch 4: The Crystal Covenant (4,000 years ago)**
- A group of Ash-Kin found a massive crystal at the center of the Dungeon's Core.
- The crystal was the System's first *node*—a physical anchor for the cosmic tutorial.
- The Ash-Kin made a pact with the crystal: they would become its shepherds, guarding the Dungeon's secrets.
- They called themselves the Covenant of the Deep.

**Epoch 5: The Great Fall (3,500 years ago)**
- The Covenant was destroyed by a rival faction of Ash-Kin who wanted to *use* the System, not guard it.
- The crystal was shattered. Fragments scattered across the world.
- The System became unstable. Skills flickered. Monsters grew erratic.
- The Dungeon went silent for a generation.

**Epoch 6: The Weaver's Rise (3,000 years ago)**
- In the darkness, a new race emerged from the depths—the Weavers.
- They had evolved from giant spiders that fed on the shattered crystal fragments.
- The Weavers were pragmatic. They saw the System not as sacred, but as *law*—and they intended to master it.
- They built the Silk Spires, their great city of geometric perfection.

---

### The Age of Kingdoms (Civilization Emerges)

> *"Order rose from chaos. Not because it was virtuous, but because it was useful."*

**Epoch 7: The Human Exodus (2,500 years ago)**
- Humans emerged from the Dungeon's Shallows, having evolved from the Dungeon's "failed experiments."
- They were weak. They had no natural advantages. But they were *many*.
- They spread across the surface like a tide, settling in the Heartlands.
- The Ash-Kin, seeing their own fading, taught the humans the System's basic rules.

**Epoch 8: The Root-Gnawer Schism (2,000 years ago)**
- A faction of humans, radicalized by the destruction of the Covenant, broke away.
- They sought *pruning*—a return to the world before civilization, before the System.
- They retreated into the Ashlands and became the Root-Gnawers.
- They began their long war against all civilization.

**Epoch 9: The Ashen War (1,500 years ago)**
- Vorthax, the Ashen King, rose from the Blightmarsh (then a fertile valley).
- He was a dragon—or rather, he had *become* a dragon through a forbidden Authority.
- He sought to consume the System's fragments and become a god.
- The Covenant of the Deep (now scattered) formed the Ashen Covenant—the knights who would stop him.
- After a brutal war, they slew Vorthax. But his dying blood poisoned the valley, creating the Blightmarsh.

---

### The Age of Crisis (Present Day)

> *"The world is stable. The world is decaying. The world is waiting."*

**Epoch 10: The Gleaming Throne (300 years ago)**
- King Aldric the Uniter forged the human capital of Aethelgard.
- He built it with Weaver silk-reinforced mortar, making it impregnable.
- The Crown was born. And with it, bureaucracy, taxation, and *control*.

**Epoch 11: The Cult's Resurgence (50 years ago)**
- The Chorus of Stillbone was founded by a fallen priest of The Observer.
- They discovered the Dragon's Grave and began their long, patient work.
- They believe that undeath is the *ultimate evolutionary state*—the end of conflict, the end of suffering.

**Epoch 12: The Present Day**
- The world is a powder keg.
- The Blightmarsh is expanding. The Cult is preparing its ritual.
- The Factions are at odds. The Crown is weak. The Root-Gnawers are growing bolder.
- The Dungeon is awakening. The System is restless.
- The player enters.

## PART 2: DWARF FORTRESS-STYLE CIVILIZATION GENERATION

> *"Every civilization is a story. Every story is a tragedy waiting to happen."*

### Civilization Generation System

When the world is created, the game generates **multiple civilizations** with the following traits:

| Trait | Description | Examples |
|-------|-------------|----------|
| **Origin** | How they were founded | *Old Kingdom* (centuries old), *Refugee Band* (fled disaster), *Colony* (offshoot of larger power) |
| **Government** | How they are ruled | *Monarchy*, *Oligarchy*, *Theocracy*, *Council*, *Anarchy* |
| **Values** | What they prioritize | *Wealth*, *War*, *Knowledge*, *Piety*, *Mercy*, *Cruelty* |
| **Resources** | What they have access to | *Iron*, *Wood*, *Magic*, *Silk*, *Ash* |
| **Grudges** | Who they hate | *Faction X* (historical grievance), *Faction Y* (resource competition) |
| **Alliances** | Who they trust | *Faction Z* (shared enemy), *Faction W* (trade partners) |
| **Crisis** | What is threatening them | *Famine*, *Civil War*, *Dungeon Uprising*, *Leadership Crisis* |

### Sample Civilization — The Kingdom of Aethelgard

```
CIVILIZATION: AETHELGARD
TYPE: Old Kingdom (300 years)
GOVERNMENT: Monarchy (Decaying)
VALUES: Wealth, Order, Control

INTERNAL FACTION: The Forge-Mother's Forge (wants to overthrow the Crown)
```

## PART 3: DYNAMIC WORLD EVENTS — DWARF FORTRESS STYLE

> *"Losing is fun. Losing is history. Losing is a story that will be told for generations."*

### Event Type 1: Civil War

**Trigger:** A civilization's internal faction reaches a critical mass of power.

**Example:** The Forge-Mother's Forge overthrows the Crown.

| Phase | Duration | State | World Signs | Player Effects |
|-------|----------|-------|-------------|----------------|
| 1 | 5-10 days | **Whispers** — Faction leaders meet secretly | NPCs speak of "change coming," nobles act nervous | Hints from black market, hidden quests |
| 2 | 3-5 days | **The Spark** — An assassination, a public execution, a riot | Faction troops march in the streets, smoke rises | Fighting in the streets, blockaded areas |
| 3 | 1-3 days | **The Siege** — The city is at war | Barricades, burning buildings, constant battle | Player must choose sides (or flee) |
| 4 | Days to weeks | **The Aftermath** — New ruler, new regime | Executions, celebrations, rebuilding | Reputation changes, new faction leaders, quest changes |

**Branching Outcomes:**

| Outcome | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Crown Wins** | Status quo maintained, but weakened. The Forge-Mother's Forge is destroyed or driven underground. |
| **Forge Wins** | New militaristic regime. The Crown is overthrown. Adventurers are now soldiers. |
| **Stalemate** | City is divided. Two regimes exist side by side. Constant skirmishes. |
| **Third Party Wins** | A third faction (e.g., The Church of The Observer) seizes power during the chaos. |
| **Collapse** | The city falls. Refugees flee. Aethelgard becomes a ruin. |

---

### Event Type 2: Faction Annihilation

**Trigger:** One civilization conquers another, or a civilization collapses from internal strife.

**Example:** The Root-Gnawers finally succeed in destroying the Verdant Valley.

| Phase | Duration | State | World Signs | Player Effects |
|-------|----------|-------|-------------|----------------|
| 1 | 10-20 days | **Harvest** — The Root-Gnawers release a new, deadlier blight | Crops wither instantly, farmers panic | Food prices skyrocket, starvation begins |
| 2 | 5-10 days | **Exodus** — The valley's population flees | Refugee caravans, abandoned villages | Overcrowded cities, disease spreads |
| 3 | 1-5 days | **The Burning** — The Root-Gnawers burn the valley | Smoke visible for 100 miles, ash rains down | The valley is uninhabitable for generations |
| 4 | Weeks to months | **Aftermath** — The valley is a wasteland | Survivors form diaspora, new factions emerge | New quests: rebuild, retaliate, or abandon |

**Branching Outcomes:**

| Outcome | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Valley Survives** | Player or factions stop the blight. The valley recovers. |
| **Valley Falls** | The valley is gone. Three kingdoms starve. |
| **Root-Gnawers Fall** | Player or factions destroy the Root-Gnawers. The valley is saved. |
| **Third Party** | Another faction (e.g., The Weavers) intervenes, saving the valley in exchange for control. |

---

### Event Type 3: Dungeon Uprising

**Trigger:** The Dungeon's monsters become more aggressive, spilling onto the surface.

**Example:** A Monster Rex escapes Floor 17 and rampages across the Heartlands.

| Phase | Duration | State | World Signs | Player Effects |
|-------|----------|-------|-------------|----------------|
| 1 | 3-5 days | **Rumbles** — Unusual tremors, monsters seen near the Fissure | Dungeon Divers return with horrific tales | Adventurer's Guild is on high alert |
| 2 | 1-3 days | **The Breach** — A Monster Rex emerges from the Fissure | A giant appears on the surface, destruction begins | Quests to stop it, cities under siege |
| 3 | 1-5 days | **The Ram** page | The Monster Rex rampages across the landscape | Multiple settlements attacked, factions unite to stop it |
| 4 | Variable | **The Hunt** — Adventurers and armies hunt the Monster Rex | A massive hunt party, the Monster Rex is tracked | Player can join the hunt, or watch |
| 5 | Aftermath | **The Aftermath** — The Monster Rex is slain (or not) | Ruins, deaths, heroes celebrated | The world is permanently changed |

**Branching Outcomes:**

| Outcome | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Monster Rex Slain** | The creature is defeated. Heroes emerge. The Dungeon respects the victory. |
| **Monster Rex Survives** | The creature is driven back to the Dungeon. It becomes a recurring threat. |
| **Multiple Exits** | More Monster Rexes emerge. A full-scale Dungeon uprising begins. |
| **The Xenos** | The sentient monsters intervene, either helping or hindering. |

---

### Event Type 4: Ecological Catastrophe

**Trigger:** A civilization's resource extraction causes irreversible environmental damage.

**Example:** Frostholme's deep-mining awakens something that poisons the surrounding lands.

| Phase | Duration | State | World Signs | Player Effects |
|-------|----------|-------|-------------|----------------|
| 1 | 10-30 days | **Digging Deeper** — Frostholme's miners go too deep | Strange sounds from the depths, glowing rocks | Dwarven NPCs become anxious |
| 2 | 5-10 days | **The Awakening** — Something ancient and terrible is released | A dark gas rises from the mines, poisoning the surface | Wildlife dies, crops wither, sickness spreads |
| 3 | 1-5 days | **The Spreading** — The poison spreads beyond Frostholme | The gas cloud expands, villages are evacuated | Entire regions become uninhabitable |
| 4 | Weeks to months | **Aftermath** — The land is permanently scarred | Refugees, new wastelands, lost history | New quests: find a cure, seal the mines, or flee |

**Branching Outcomes:**

| Outcome | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **The Mine is Sealed** | The Dwarves seal the deep mine, but the poison remains. |
| **The Poison is Cured** | A cure is found (possibly from the Root-Gnawers' knowledge). |
| **The Evil Emerges** | The poison is the precursor to something worse—a new enemy emerges. |
| **Frostholme Falls** | The city is abandoned. The Dwarves become a nomadic people. |

---

### Event Type 5: Religious Schism

**Trigger:** A god's followers split over an interpretation of their doctrine.

**Example:** The Church of The Observer splits into two sects—the "Record Keepers" and the "Interventionists."

| Phase | Duration | State | World Signs | Player Effects |
|-------|----------|-------|-------------|----------------|
| 1 | 5-15 days | **The Debate** — Priests argue over doctrine | Religious NPCs speak of "heresy" and "revelation" | Quests to gather holy texts |
| 2 | 3-5 days | **The Schism** — The Church divides | Two churches, two doctrines, two leaders | Player must choose a side (or stay neutral) |
| 3 | 1-3 days | **The Conflict** — Violence erupts between the sects | Riots, burning churches, assassinations | The city is divided; loyalties are tested |
| 4 | Aftermath | **The Aftermath** — One sect wins, or both survive | New doctrine, new power structures | Reputation changes, new quests |

**Branching Outcomes:**

| Outcome | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Record Keepers Win** | The Church remains passive. The Interventionists are exiled. |
| **Interventionists Win** | The Church becomes active. They start intervening in worldly affairs. |
| **Both Survive** | Two churches exist. Future conflict is inevitable. |
| **Third Party** | A new sect emerges, combining both doctrines. |

---

### Event Type 6: Hero Emergence

**Trigger:** A random NPC performs a heroic act and becomes a famous figure.

**Example:** A farmer's daughter discovers she has a Unique Skill and begins fighting monsters.

| Phase | Duration | State | World Signs | Player Effects |
|-------|----------|-------|-------------|----------------|
| 1 | 1-3 days | **The Deed** — A notable event occurs | NPCs speak of a "miracle" or "heroic act" | The hero's name starts spreading |
| 2 | 3-10 days | **The Rise** — The hero gains followers | The hero appears in public, gathering attention | Quests to meet the hero, join their cause |
| 3 | 10-30 days | **The Quest** — The hero undertakes a major quest | The hero's exploits are celebrated | The hero may ask for the player's help |
| 4 | Aftermath | **The Aftermath** — The hero succeeds or fails | The hero becomes a legend or a tragedy | The world is changed; the hero becomes a faction leader |

**Branching Outcomes:**

| Outcome | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Hero Succeeds** | The hero achieves their goal. They become a powerful ally. |
| **Hero Fails** | The hero dies or is broken. Their followers mourn (or seek revenge). |
| **Hero Turns** | The hero is corrupted. They become a villain. |
| **Player Influences** | The player can mentor, ally with, or oppose the hero. |

### Event Type 7: Forgotten Beast Awakening

**Trigger:** A deep exploration uncovers a forgotten beast from a past age.

**Example:** A miner in the Ashlands unearths the "Soul-Eating Worm"—a creature from the Age of Embers.

| Phase | Duration | State | World Signs | Player Effects |
|-------|----------|-------|-------------|----------------|
| 1 | 1-3 days | **Discovery** — Something ancient is found | NPCs speak of "something terrible" or "something wondrous" | Quests to investigate the discovery |
| 2 | 1-5 days | **The Awakening** — The beast awakens | Tremors, strange weather, nightmares | The beast is seen (or felt) by many |
| 3 | Variable | **The Hunt** — Adventurers and armies hunt the beast | Massive expeditions, deaths, chaos | Player can join the hunt |
| 4 | Aftermath | **The Aftermath** — The beast is slain (or not) | Legendary rewards, permanent changes to the landscape | The world is marked by the beast's passage |

**Branching Outcomes:**

| Outcome | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Beast Slain** | The creature is destroyed. Its parts become legendary materials. |
| **Beast Survives** | The creature retreats to a hidden lair. It becomes a recurring threat. |
| **Beast is Befriended** | The creature is tamed (if Xenos-like). It becomes a powerful ally. |
| **Beast Escapes** | The creature leaves the region. It may appear elsewhere. |

## PART 4: WORLD EVENT CASCADES

> *"One event is a story. Two events are a pattern. Three events are a world."*

### Cascade Example 1: The Fall of Frostholme

```
EVENT A: The Dwarves dig too deep (Ecological Catastrophe)
    ↓
EVENT B: The deep-mining awakens a Forgotten Beast (Forgotten Beast Awakening)
    ↓
EVENT C: The Beast destroys Frostholme (Faction Annihilation)
    ↓
EVENT D: The Dwarven refugees flood into Aethelgard (Population Migration)
    ↓
EVENT E: The refugees bring a strange sickness (Plague Event)
    ↓
EVENT F: The Crown blames the refugees, causing a Civil War (Civil War)
```

### Cascade Example 2: The Root-Gnawer Resurgence

```
EVENT A: The Root-Gnawers release a new blight (Ecological Catastrophe)
    ↓
EVENT B: The Verdant Valley starves (Ecological Catastrophe)
    ↓
EVENT C: The Crown blames the Weavers (Religious Schism)
    ↓
EVENT D: War breaks out between the Crown and the Weavers (Civil War)
    ↓
EVENT E: The Root-Gnawers take advantage of the chaos (Faction Annihilation)
    ↓
EVENT F: The world is plunged into a new dark age (Dark Age Event)
```

### Cascade Example 3: The Dragon's Return

```
EVENT A: The Chorus of Stillbone gathers power (Religious Schism)
    ↓
EVENT B: The cult awakens Vorthax (Dungeon Uprising)
    ↓
EVENT C: Vorthax rampages across the Heartlands (Faction Annihilation)
    ↓
EVENT D: The Ashen Covenant reforms (Hero Emergence)
    ↓
EVENT E: The Covenant and the Cult clash (Civil War)
    ↓
EVENT F: The player must choose a side (Player Decision Point)
```

## PART 5: WORLD MEMORY & HISTORY SYSTEM

> *"The world remembers. The world forgets. The world tells stories."*

### History Storage System

Every major event is stored in the **World History Database**:

| Field | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| **Event ID** | Unique identifier |
| **Event Type** | Civil War, Faction Annihilation, Dungeon Uprising, etc. |
| **Date** | In-game timestamp |
| **Location** | Where it happened |
| **Actors** | Who was involved |
| **Outcome** | What happened |
| **Consequences** | How the world was changed |
| **Legacy** | The story that is told about this event |

### History Generation Example

```
EVENT ID: E-0001
TYPE: Civilization Founding
DATE: Year 1, Age of Kingdoms
LOCATION: Frostholme
ACTORS: The Dwarven People
OUTCOME: Frostholme was founded.
CONSEQUENCES: The Dwarves became a major power in the Northern Reaches.
LEGACY: "The Mountain-Father carved a home from stone, and the Dwarves became his children."

EVENT ID: E-0142
TYPE: Dungeon Uprising
DATE: Year 143, Age of Kingdoms
LOCATION: The Outer Fissure
ACTORS: The Dungeon Divers, The Black Terror (Monster Rex)
OUTCOME: The Black Terror was slain, but it cost 50 lives.
CONSEQUENCES: The Divers became a formal organization.
LEGACY: "The Black Terror's bones now hang in the guild hall. We do not forget."

EVENT ID: E-0289
TYPE: Civil War
DATE: Year 289, Age of Kingdoms
LOCATION: Aethelgard
ACTORS: The Crown, The Forge-Mother's Forge
OUTCOME: The Crown won, but was weakened.
CONSEQUENCES: The Forge-Mother's Forge went underground.
LEGACY: "The Forge-Mother's children still walk among us. They have not forgotten."
```

### History's Mechanical Effect

- **Faction Memory:** Factions remember events that affected them. This affects their behavior (trust, hostility, alliances).
- **NPC Memory:** NPCs remember events they witnessed. This affects their dialogue, quests, and relationships.
- **Cultural Memory:** Events become part of a civilization's culture. This affects their values, goals, and symbols.
- **Player Memory:** The player can access the history database, learning about the world's past. This can unlock hidden quests and knowledge.

## PART 6: NPC AGENTS — DWARF FORTRESS STYLE

> *"Every NPC is a person. Every person has a story. Every story is a tragedy waiting to happen."*

### NPC Agent System

NPCs are generated with the following traits:

| Trait | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| **Name** | Generated from racial naming conventions |
| **Race** | Human, Beastfolk, Ash-Kin, etc. |
| **Occupation** | Farmer, Soldier, Merchant, Scholar, etc. |
| **Personality** | Brave, Cowardly, Greedy, Generous, etc. |
| **Goals** | What they want to achieve (short and long term) |
| **Relationships** | Family, friends, rivals, enemies |
| **Status** | Alive, Dead, Missing, etc. |
| **History** | Notable events they've experienced |

### Sample NPC

```
NPC: ELARA VEX
Race: Human
Occupation: Captain of the Millford Militia
Personality: Pragmatic, Weary, Haunted
Goals: Protect Millford, Resist the Cult, Survive
Relationships: 
    - Daughter of the Ashen Covenant (bloodline)
    - Rival of Merrik the Silent (enemy)
    - Respects the player (if they help)
Status: Alive
History:
    - Watched her father die in the first ashfall
    - Has held off two cult assaults
    - Dreams of Vorthax's return
```

### NPC Simulation

NPCs in Genesis follow a Dwarf Fortress-style simulation:

1. **Goal Selection:** Each NPC evaluates their goals based on their personality, status, and environment.
2. **Planning:** NPCs generate plans to achieve their goals (GOAP system).
3. **Execution:** NPCs execute their plans in real time.
4. **Memory:** NPCs remember events and learn from them.
5. **Evolution:** NPCs can change over time (gain new goals, develop new relationships, die).

### Example NPC Behavior

**Merrik the Silent (Lich, Cult Leader):**
1. **Goal:** Resurrect Vorthax.
2. **Plan:** Gather souls → Complete ritual → Awaken Vorthax.
3. **Execution:** Send cultists to Millford → Sacrifice souls → Ritual begins.
4. **Memory:** Remembers previous attempts (and failures).
5. **Evolution:** Will adapt if the player interferes.

**Captain Elara Vex:**
1. **Goal:** Protect Millford.
2. **Plan:** Fortify walls → Train militia → Send for aid.
3. **Execution:** Coordinates defense, patrols, prays.
4. **Memory:** Remembers the player's actions (help or hinder).
5. **Evolution:** Will become embittered (if failed) or grateful (if helped).

## PART 7: THE WORLD SYSTEM — HIDDEN MECHANICS

> *"The world is not a backdrop. It is a machine. And machines have rules."*

### The World Clock

The world operates on a hidden simulation clock:

| Tick Type | Frequency | Purpose |
|-----------|-----------|---------|
| **Combat Tick** | 0.1 seconds | Real-time combat and physics |
| **NPC Tick** | 1 second | NPC movement, dialogue, simple actions |
| **Economy Tick** | 1 hour | Resource production, trade, prices |
| **Day Tick** | 24 hours | Settlement growth, weather, NPC needs |
| **Month Tick** | 30 days | Faction actions, political events |
| **Year Tick** | 365 days | Civilizations, wars, major events |

### The Hidden Variables

The world has hidden variables that affect everything:

| Variable | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| **World Stability** | How stable the world is (chaos vs. order) |
| **Dungeon Activity** | How active the Dungeon is (dormant vs. furious) |
| **God Attention** | How much the gods are watching (uninterested vs. fascinated) |
| **Crisis Potential** | The likelihood of major events occurring |
| **Faction Trust** | How much factions trust each other |
| **Resource Scarcity** | How scarce resources are (global) |

### The Cascade Algorithm

Events cascade when multiple variables align:

```
STABILITY LOW + DUNGEON ACTIVITY HIGH + CRISIS POTENTIAL HIGH → 
    → MONSTER REX UPRISING → 
        → FACTION TRUST LOW → 
            → NO ALLIANCE AGAINST THE THREAT → 
                → CIVILIZATION COLLAPSES → 
                    → WORLD STABILITY DROPS FURTHER
```

## PART 8: EVENT CHAIN TEMPLATES

### Template 1: The Food Crisis

| Phase | Description | World Signs | Player Effects |
|-------|-------------|-------------|----------------|
| 1 | **Shortage** — A blight, a drought, or a war disrupts food production | Food prices rise, NPCs complain | Basic quests: find food, secure supply lines |
| 2 | **Famine** — Food becomes scarce | Hunger, riots, refugee camps | Morality quests: help or hoard |
| 3 | **Starvation** — People begin to die | Deaths, desperation, cannibalism | Hero quests: lead a relief expedition |
| 4 | **Collapse** — The affected civilization falls | Refugees, new factions, wastelands | Rebuilding quests: found a new settlement |

### Template 2: The Monster Siege

| Phase | Description | World Signs | Player Effects |
|-------|-------------|-------------|----------------|
| 1 | **Rumors** — Monsters are seen gathering | NPCs speak of fear, adventurers return with stories | Investigation quests |
| 2 | **The Siege Begins** — Monsters attack a settlement | Walls breached, people die, smoke rises | Combat quests: defend the settlement |
| 3 | **The Retreat** — If the settlement falls, monsters advance | Refugees, burning villages | Retreat quests: guide survivors |
