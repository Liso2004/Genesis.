PART 1: EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Project Genesis is a third-person action RPG and living-world simulation. Its identity combines Trial-based skill mastery—inspired by The Tutorial Is Too Hard and That Time I Got Reincarnated as a Slime—with emergent civilization simulation inspired by WorldBox. The world continues independently of the player while creating opportunities for exploration, mastery, relationships, conflict, and legacy.

Core Pillars
Pillar	Description
Living World	Civilizations, economies, politics, ecology, and history continue without the player.
Mastery	Skills improve through meaningful use, experimentation, and pressure.
Transformative Trials	Challenges force genuine capability and principle mastery.
Emergent Quests	Simulation events become opportunities rather than static quest-board entries.
Persistent Consequence	Important actions leave traces in people, factions, settlements, and history.
Director	An invisible orchestration layer observes patterns and curates challenges without cheating.
The Dungeon	A living, sentient labyrinth that teaches through themed floors—the world's primary classroom.
Inspirations
Inspiration	Taken	Genesis Distinction
The Tutorial Is Too Hard	Trials, themed floors, mastery	Extended beyond a fixed tutorial into a living world
That Time I Got Reincarnated as a Slime	Unique Skills (Great Sage), Sin/Virtue series	Every player gets a random Unique Skill; full Sin/Holy series
DanMachi	Living dungeon, monster spawning, Safe Zones, Xenos	Added pedagogical floor design; world-dungeon connection
WorldBox	Emergent civilizations and world simulation	Added deep individual NPCs and adaptive Trials
Elden Ring	Exploration and environmental storytelling	World-state evolution as discovery driver
RimWorld / Dwarf Fortress	Emergent stories	3D action-RPG presentation
Core Design Laws
The world exists independently of the player.

The Director curates; it does not fabricate reality.

Every meaningful Trial should teach or reinforce a principle.

Every meaningful quest should leave the world or player changed.

Skills strengthen through meaningful use and mastery.

Failure creates history rather than automatically erasing it.

NPCs pursue goals based on state, memory, and relationships.

Economies arise from production, scarcity, demand, risk, and trade.

Gods and Authorities are world systems, not reward menus.

Curiosity is rewarded.

PART 2: DESIGN CONSTITUTION
Engineering Standards
Prefer data-driven content.

Use ScriptableObjects for authored definitions.

Separate simulation from presentation.

Use versioned persistent state and migration.

Profile before scaling simulation.

Every major system needs tests and documentation.

AI Standards
Utility scoring for goal selection.

GOAP for multi-step planning where useful.

Behavior Trees for local execution where useful.

Relevance tiers for distant NPCs.

The Director may not silently invalidate agency.

Unity Standards
GameObjects for authored/presentation-heavy gameplay.

ECS/Entities only where profiling justifies scale.

Addressables/streaming for large content.

Assembly Definitions aligned to Genesis modules.

Runtime state must not be stored in shared content assets.

PART 3: COSMOLOGY — THE COSMIC TUTORIAL
"The universe is a classroom. You are the student. The lesson is existence."

The Origin of the System
The universe was created by a higher-order being—not a god, but something beyond gods. A being of pure curiosity who wanted to see what would happen if beings were given the capacity to grow without limit.

This being created the System as a framework for growth. Stats, skills, levels, and Authorities are not arbitrary—they are a language that reality speaks. Understanding that language allows mastery over existence.

The System is not a UI invention. It is a physical law of this universe. Living beings have "Essence"—cosmic potential that can be shaped by experience. Skills are patterns of Essence usage. Authorities are fundamental laws of reality bound to a living being.

The Purpose
The System exists to force transformation. Without it, beings would be static. With it, they can evolve, adapt, and transcend.

The ultimate goal is to create beings capable of breaking the rules entirely—achieving Authority, and eventually, becoming something beyond even that.

The Director's Role
The Director is the curriculum designer—the invisible teacher who observes, evaluates, and curates opportunities for growth.

The Director does not cheat. It does not guarantee success or failure. It simply ensures that opportunities exist.

The Dungeon's Role
The Dungeon is the primary classroom—the place where the System's lessons are made manifest in flesh and stone.

Every floor, every monster, every trap is a lesson. The Dungeon does not kill—it teaches through consequence.

The Gods' Role
Gods are advanced students—beings who have achieved Authority and chosen to remain in the world to guide others.

They are not omnipotent. They are bound by the System, just like mortals. They simply understand it better.

They may reward, test, ignore, oppose, or manipulate—but they cannot break the rules.

PART 5: THE DUNGEON — THE CRADLE OF TRIALS
"The Dungeon does not hate you. It teaches you. The question is whether you are willing to learn."

Design Philosophy
The Dungeon is the primary engine of growth in Project Genesis. It is a living, sentient ecosystem that exists to challenge, refine, and transform those who enter it. Drawing inspiration from DanMachi's labyrinthine underworld and The Tutorial Is Too Hard's pedagogical floor design, the Dungeon serves as both a proving ground for mortals and a cosmic classroom.

Core Principles
The Dungeon is alive. Spawns monsters, repairs itself, reacts to intruders.

The Dungeon teaches. Each floor introduces new mechanics, forcing adaptation.

The Dungeon remembers. Tracks who has entered, what they've learned, and how they've grown.

The Dungeon is the world's curriculum. The Director curates opportunities; the Dungeon executes them.

Theme: The Gauntlet
Lesson: "The world is predictable, if you know how to read it."

Scenario: A 100-meter corridor of pale blue stone. The walls are lined with arrow slits. The floor is perfectly flat. At the end is a locked iron door. The key is at the end of the corridor, but it is covered in a pressure plate.

Mechanics:

Arrow Pattern: Left, Right, Center, Low, High (accelerating)

Pressure Plate: Key sits on plate; taking it triggers instant volley

Side Paths: Small alcoves offer temporary safety

Enemies:

Stone Golems (x3): Slow-moving, high HP

Boss: The Arrow Warden
Massive skeleton with bow; pattern: Arm Raise → Red Glow → Fire (3 arrows)

Win Condition: Reach the door, unlock it, exit.
Failure State: Death → respawn at Floor 1 entry corridor.

Waiting Room: Floor 1 Entry. Stone chamber with healing fountain and merchant.

War Shadows: Manifestations of guilt and regret

Floor 3: The Siege of Clearwater
Theme: The Siege of Innocence

NPC AI: Commands: Follow, Guard Here, Fetch Supplies

Morale: NPCs lose morale if allies die

Massive goblin on ram.

Win Condition: Defeat all waves, protect at least 3/5 NPCs.
Failure State: Village burns; player proceeds but learns cost of failure.

Lesson: "Trust your senses. Trust your instincts. Trust nothing else."

Scenario: Giant maze of polished obsidian mirrors. Walls shift silently. Player's reflection sometimes moves independently.

Cyclops that sees through illusions; stomps to reveal paths.

Mechanics:

Poisoned Dishes: Sleep, Paralysis, Hallucination, Death (one safe dish)

Observation: Watch butler's gestures

Boss: The Feast Master
Massive demon; attacks with bile and telekinesis.

Win Condition: Eat safe food, defeat Feast Master.
Failure State: Poisoned (death). Respawn at Floor 4.

Waiting Room: FLOOR 5 SAFE ZONE. Beautiful garden with healing fountain, merchant.

Floor 6: The River of Sludge
Theme: The Flooded Crypt
Lesson: "Panic is a silent killer. Breathe. Plan. Survive."

Scenario: Massive underground vault slowly filling with black toxic sludge. Player must navigate debris, climb pillars, find drain valves.

Mechanics:

Toxic Water: 10 damage/second

Valve System: Three valves; activating all drains the room

Giant elemental; can only be hurt when sludge level below 50%.

Floor 7: The Gambler's Den
Theme: The Gambler's Trial
Lesson: "Sometimes, victory is not a matter of skill, but a matter of odds."

Combat Option: Fight thugs, but they get stronger over time

Masked figure; deals cards as attacks; player must "call" the suit.

PART 6: SKILLS & AUTHORITIES SYSTEM
Skill Philosophy
"Skills represent learned capability. Variation and application matter, not only repetition."

Progression: Exposure → Practice → Competence → Confidence → Mastery → Innovation

Skill Tier System
Tier	Name	Description	Example
1	Basic	Learned through practice. Usable but unrefined.	Sword Swing — 10 damage, 5 stamina
2	Extra	Refined through mastery. Can be performed instinctively.	Flowing Sword — 15 damage, chain attacks
3	Unique	Personalized through deep mastery. No one else uses it the same way.	Flowing Sword of Silence — chain attacks with silence effect
4	Ultimate	Defines the user. Peak mortal capability. Can bend local reality.	Flowing Sword of Absolute Stillness — freeze movement in 10m radius
5	Authority	Beyond mortal capability. Tied to cosmic systems. Alters rules of existence.	Authority of Sword: The Edge That Cuts Worlds — cut anything, including concepts
Skill Categories
Active: Skills that are actively used (attacks, spells, abilities).

Passive: Skills that are always active (resistances, stat boosts).

Mastery: Skills that represent deep understanding of a domain.

Resistance: Skills that protect against specific damage types.

Gifted/Knowledge: Skills that represent innate talent or learned knowledge.

Emergent: Skills that arise from novel combinations.

Skill Evolution Requirements
Evolution	Requirement
Basic → Extra	100+ uses + successful application in varied contexts
Extra → Unique	10+ critical situations + 5+ creative applications + personal breakthrough
Unique → Ultimate	3+ existential crises + mastery of core principle + Trial
Ultimate → Authority	Cosmic resonance + Trial of Understanding + embodiment of principle
PART 7: UNIQUE SKILLS — THE 100+ BIRTHRIGHTS
"The System does not give you power. It gives you potential. What you become is up to you."

Design Philosophy
Every player begins with a random Unique Skill awakened by the System based on the player's soul, personality, and potential. Inspired by Rimuru Tempest's Great Sage.

Core Principles:

Every player is unique. No two players start with the same Unique Skill.

Skills grow with the player. Unique Skills evolve through meaningful use.

Skills reflect the soul. A manifestation of deepest nature.

Skills can become Authorities. Evolution through Ultimate → God Series.

Unique Skill Evolution Path
Tier	Name	Description
1	Extra Skill	The seed of potential. Awakened during reincarnation or crisis.
2	Unique Skill	The skill fully manifests. Has clear function and personality.
3	Ultimate Skill	Evolves into a world-altering power. Gains a "Lord" title.
4	Ultimate Skill (Sin/Virtue Series)	Aligns with a cosmic principle.
5	God Series (Authority)	Becomes a fundamental law of reality.
Unique Skill Awakening
When a player enters the world for the first time, they experience the Awakening:

"The Voice of the World speaks:
"Your soul has been recognized. Your potential has been measured. Your Unique Skill awakens."

The player receives a random Unique Skill from the pool of 100+, tailored to their personality, choices, and "resonance" of their soul.

PART 12: THE LIVING WORLD — EVENT CHAINS
Event Chain Philosophy
The world outside the Dungeon is alive. While the player explores, the world continues to evolve independently.

Core Principles:

The world is the test. What the player learns in the Dungeon is applied outside.

The world is alive. It progresses independently of the player.

The world remembers. Actions—or inaction—have consequences.

The world is connected. Events in the Dungeon affect the world, and vice versa.

Sample Event Chain: "The Ashen Cradle"
Premise: The Chorus of Stillbone cult has been gathering power in the Blightmarsh. They have located Vorthax's remains and plan to resurrect him. The ritual requires the sacrifice of Millford—the descendants of those who slew him.

Phase 0 — "Whispers in the Ash" (Incipient)

Duration: 3-5 days

State: Cult cell activity increases. Grave robbing reports spike.

World Signs: Ash falls near the marsh. Livestock grow sickly.

Player Intervention (Early): Investigate → find cult scouts → disrupt supply lines → slow ritual.

Phase 1 — "The Wound Opens" (Escalation)

Duration: 7-10 days

State: Cult awakens Vorthax's spirit. Blightmarsh expands. Millford sits in the marsh's path.

World Signs: Ash falls daily. Crops fail. River turns brackish. Prices spike.

Player Intervention (Mid): Aid evacuation, reinforce defenses, or investigate marsh.

Phase 2 — "The Ashen King Rises" (Crisis)

Duration: 3-5 days

State: Cult completes ritual. Vorthax rises as undead abomination.

World Signs: Black smoke pillar visible for miles. Sky darkens. Refugees flood cities.

Player Intervention (Late): Ground combat → aerial assault → destroy heart-orb.

Phase 3 — "The Aftermath" (Resolution)

State: Vorthax is defeated (or not).

Consequences: Blightmarsh becomes permanent wasteland. Refugees form diaspora. Player's legend shaped by intervention.

Branching Outcomes:

Intervention Point	Outcome
Early	Ritual delayed. Millford survives. Vorthax rises weaker.
Mid (Evacuate)	Population saved. Millford becomes ghost town. Vorthax rises at full power.
Mid (Reinforce)	Population survives siege. Vorthax rises at full power but slowed.
Mid (Investigate)	Player can interrupt ritual or let it complete with sabotage.
Late	Massive devastation. Player inherits guilt of every death.
Never	Simulation resolves. Vorthax rampages for 30-50 days.
Sample Event Chain: "The Silent Harvest"
Premise: A mysterious blight has begun consuming grain stores across the Verdant Valley—a biological weapon released by the Root-Gnawers.

Phase 0 — "The First Withered Leaf" (Incipient)

Duration: 5-8 days

State: A few farms report crop failure. Authorities dismiss it.

World Signs: Grain prices stable. A few merchants begin hoarding.

Player Intervention (Early): Investigate sick farms → trace blight → destroy sect or negotiate.

Phase 1 — "The Empty Silo" (Escalation)

Duration: 15-20 days

State: Blight spreads to 40% of valley. Factions hoard grain. Trade routes disrupted.

World Signs: Grain prices triple. Bread scarce. Families eat seed corn.

Player Intervention (Mid): Broker agreement, secure alternatives, or expedite cure.

Phase 2 — "The Wages of Starvation" (Crisis)

Duration: 20-30 days

State: Blight covers 80% of valley. Famine widespread. One kingdom collapses.

World Signs: Corpses in streets. Cannibalism. Faction leaders dead or hiding.

Player Intervention (Late): Forced harvest, faction unification, or total abandonment.

Branching Outcomes:

Intervention Point	Outcome
Early (Destroy Sect)	Blight ends. Cure never discovered. Cult martyred.
Early (Negotiate)	Blight cured slowly. Sect becomes faction.
Mid (Broker Agreement)	Factions cooperate. "Grain League" formed.
Mid (Secure Alternatives)	Some populations survive. Valley becomes more urbanized.
Late (Forced Harvest)	Forest devastated. Cure saves kingdoms. Ecological cost.
Late (Faction Unification)	Single kingdom emerges. Authoritarian but efficient.
Late (Mass Exodus)	Valley abandoned. Survivors disperse.
Never	Valley collapses. Three kingdoms fall. New dark age.
PART 13: THE DIRECTOR SYSTEM
Definition
The Director is the curriculum designer—the invisible teacher who observes, evaluates, and curates opportunities for growth.

Responsibilities
Responsibility	Description
Teacher	Create opportunities to learn.
Curator	Select meaningful opportunities supported by simulation.
Historian	Preserve important consequences.
Inputs
Player behavior and patterns

Skill usage/mastery

Relationships and reputation

World-state changes

Quest opportunities

Faction and political conditions

Economy and resources

Historical threads

Divine/Authority resonance

Player Model
The Director maintains a behavioral profile:

Observation

Patience

Leadership

Compassion

Risk Tolerance

Creativity

Discipline

Curiosity

Ambition

Hard Constraints
Never guarantee success or failure.

Never silently rewrite history for drama.

Never protect favorite NPCs solely for narrative reasons.

Never spawn enemies purely because a level number increased.

Never remove meaningful consequences without a world reason.

Preserve agency.

Technical Model
The Director consumes world-state snapshots/events and outputs candidate opportunities with scores and constraints. A validator checks whether an opportunity is actually possible before activation.
